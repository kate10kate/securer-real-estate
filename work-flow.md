# SecureRealEstate Web2 — Complete Workflow, Branches and Protocol Rules

Version 1 · Business logic and authorization specification · No code implementation

SecureRealEstate is a Web2 investment application where investors purchase fractional shares in properties, real estate companies raise funds, and approved funds are released in development milestones. Investors can receive dividends, while SecureRealEstate earns a purchase fee and a share of property dividends.

The protocol's job is to enforce the numbers, ownership, authorization, financial records and permitted state transitions. The website displays the information and collects requests; it must not be trusted to calculate balances or decide whether an operation is allowed.

## 1. The financial model

There are three important money flows.

A. Investor purchases shares

The investor pays one amount. SecureRealEstate deducts 1% from that payment, and the remaining 99% is credited toward the investor's property share purchase.

B. Fundraising and development

Funds are accounted for against the property offering. After fundraising closes, the system allocates the funds to three milestones and a restricted reserve.

C. Property dividends

When a property pays a dividend, SecureRealEstate receives 10% of the total dividend, and the remaining 90% is distributed among eligible investors in proportion to their shares.

### Rule 1 — The 1% investor purchase fee

The 1% fee comes out of the money the investor pays, not on top of it.

For an investor payment of ₦100,000:

| Calculation                               | Amount   |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Total amount paid                         | ₦100,000 |
| SecureRealEstate fee (1%)                 | ₦1,000   |
| Amount available for share purchase (99%) | ₦99,000  |

If the share price is ₦10,000, the investor can purchase 9 whole shares for ₦90,000, with ₦9,000 left over. The system must follow the offering's share-purchase rules for any remainder; it must not silently sell fractional shares unless fractional ownership is supported.

Important distinction: The fee is calculated on the amount paid for the purchase. The system must separately record the fee and the net investment amount. The fee is not part of the property fundraising contribution.

The purchase must be rejected or recalculated if the net amount cannot purchase the requested number of shares, if the shares are unavailable, or if the payment fails.

### Rule 2 — The 10% dividend fee

The 10% is calculated from the total dividend paid by the property, before investors receive their distributions.

| Recipient                           | Share |
| ----------------------------------- | ----- |
| SecureRealEstate                    | 10%   |
| All eligible investors collectively | 90%   |
| Total                               | 100%  |

For example, on a ₦10 million dividend payment, SecureRealEstate receives ₦1 million and investors collectively receive ₦9 million.

An investor holding 20% of all eligible shares receives 20% of the investor pool: ₦1.8 million.

The previous Web3 split of 6% to the protocol and 4% to a listing platform is replaced by 10% to SecureRealEstate, because the separate listing platform is no longer part of this model. The 10% rate should be configurable through an authorised process, with the rate used for each distribution recorded in the transaction history.

### Rules

1. A company cannot create an active fundraising offering until its registration has been approved.
2. A property offering must specify the property, share price, total shares available, fundraising target, deadline, milestone percentages, reserve percentage and dividend terms.
3. The property remains Pending until an authorised administrator approves it.
4. Approval moves the property to Fundraising.
5. A rejected offering cannot accept investor purchases.
6. Once fundraising opens, the approved share price, share supply, funding target and deadline are locked. The minimum/target cannot be changed after opening.
7. A company may request early closure, but closure is irreversible once approved.

The system should retain the approved offering terms as a versioned record. It must not silently rewrite the terms after investors have purchased shares.

### Rules

Deposits

- A deposit is credited only after the payment provider or bank confirms receipt.
- A pending, failed or duplicated payment must not create extra spendable balance.

Purchases

Before a purchase succeeds, the system checks:

- The investor's account is eligible to invest.
- The property is currently fundraising.
- The fundraising deadline has not passed and the offering has not closed.
- The requested shares are available.
- The investor has sufficient available balance.
- The share price and fee calculation use the approved offering terms.

The system then performs the purchase as one logically atomic operation: deduct the payment, allocate the fee, credit the net investment to the offering, allocate the shares and record ownership. If any required step fails, the operation must not leave a partial purchase.

Share calculations

The purchase fee is 1% of the amount paid for the purchase. The remaining 99% is the amount available to buy shares. If only whole shares are supported, the system must calculate the affordable share quantity and explicitly handle any remainder according to the purchase rules. It must never allocate more shares than are available or than the investor has paid for.

The reserve is 15% of the final accepted funding amount. It is restricted money, not a fourth ordinary milestone and not money the company can freely withdraw.

- Reserve funds remain unavailable for ordinary development spending.
- The company may request a reserve release only for a predefined emergency.
- The request must include supporting evidence.
- An authorised administrator must approve the request, and financial releases require two authorised approvers.
- The requested amount must be within the permitted cap and the available reserve balance.
- The system records the reason, evidence references, approvers, amount and actual payment result.
- The system rejects a request that is not an eligible emergency, exceeds the cap or exceeds the available reserve.

The emergency categories and per-event cap must be configured before fundraising opens. If the reserve is never needed, it remains restricted until the property's approved completion or closure rules determine its final treatment. It must not automatically become company profit.

## 12. Financial accounting rules

These are foundational. They should remain part of the protocol specification even as the website evolves.

1. Every movement is traceable. Each deposit, purchase, fee, contribution, release, dividend, refund and withdrawal has a unique reference.
2. No double spending. The same balance cannot fund two purchases or payouts. Concurrent requests must not over-allocate shares or money.
3. No invented money. A balance changes only through a verified financial event or an authorised accounting correction.
4. No silent corrections. Corrections preserve the original transaction and record who corrected it, why and when.
5. No premature success status. A request or approval is not the same as a successful payment.
6. No duplicate processing. Retried requests, repeated provider notifications and network timeouts must not create duplicate purchases, releases, fees or dividends.
7. Amounts reconcile. Every financial operation must account for the full amount, including fees, allocations, unpaid amounts and rounding.
8. Ownership reconciles. Shares sold cannot exceed the approved supply, and investor ownership must match the purchase history.
